# Greg Anti-Examples

These are explicit failure modes. Use them for critique, negative prompts, and corpus balance.

## 1. Generic gradient SaaS page
Symptoms:
- Purple-blue gradient background with no product-specific mechanism.
- Vague copy like “Scale your business with AI”.
- Feature cards full of filler adjectives.
- No proof, no metrics discipline, no credible code example.

Why it fails:
- It looks like a template, not an opinionated system.
- The copy could belong to any startup.

## 2. Opus-like “pretty but generic” landing
Symptoms:
- Clean and competent, but emotionally flat.
- Safe feature ordering and over-familiar wording.
- Limited micro-detail in tags, HUDs, live indicators, or code treatment.
- Good structure without the extra layer of narrative precision.

Why it fails:
- Greg-like work usually has more conviction, stronger proof density, and more specific technical texture.

## 3. Bland docs portal
Symptoms:
- List of links with no sense of path or priority.
- Quickstart buried below the fold.
- No distinction between guides, API, and reference material.
- Good information, weak information scent.

Why it fails:
- Greg quality still requires hierarchy and purposeful emphasis, even for utilitarian surfaces.

## 4. Over-decorated dashboard
Symptoms:
- Too many glows, badges, and charts competing at once.
- No clear operational question answered by the first view.
- Noise overwhelms status and actionability.

Why it fails:
- Greg-style polish is structured restraint, not maximal ornament.

## 5. Empty pricing table
Symptoms:
- Simple columns with no operational framing.
- No explanation of how usage, support, or deployment work.
- CTA is the only thing emphasized.

Why it fails:
- Greg-like pricing should still feel like infrastructure with real consequences.
